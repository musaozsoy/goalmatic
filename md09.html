Chapter 4 · The letter in the crate

Emre did not go back to the attic for three days. He told himself it was because of homework. It was not because of homework.

On Thursday, Babaanne sent him up for the Christmas lights, which was either very unlucky or — he suspected, watching her not look at him — very deliberate. The crate was where he had left it, lid open. The ball was where he had left it. But the notebook was not.

It lay on top of the folded blanket, closed, and tucked into its cover was an envelope that had certainly not been there before. The paper was thin and yellow, the kind that tears if you breathe on it. On the front, in Arthur's slanting pencil: For the boy who isn't from here.

Emre's hands were not quite steady as he opened it.

Dear Emre,

Ronnie says you're a dream and Peggy says you're a ghost and Mum says I am to stop talking about you at the table. I don't think you are any of those things. I think you are from somewhere I can't go, and I think the ball is how you get here, and I think that is why it goes cold.

I am writing because something has happened and I cannot tell anyone else. Last night the siren went again and we went down, and when we came up the Thompsons' house was not there. Not damaged — gone. The twins are with their aunt in Kenilworth. Mr Thompson is in the hospital. Nobody will tell me about Mrs Thompson, which means I already know.

I have been thinking that if you are from somewhere ahead of us — and I think you are — then you know things. You know what happens. I am not asking you to tell me, because I don't think you're allowed, and because I'm not sure I want you to. I am asking you to do one thing, and I would be forever in your debt: keep the ball safe. Whatever happens to me, keep it safe, and keep it warm, and one day bring it back to Vine Street.

Your friend, I hope,
Arthur Bailey

Emre read it twice. Then he sat for a long time with the letter in one hand and the ball in the other, and the ball — he was almost sure — was a little warmer than the attic.

Downstairs, Babaanne called up that the lights could wait, and that there was tea.

Q5 · In your notebook · 2 marks

How can you tell that Arthur is frightened but trying to stay calm in his letter? Point → because → "quotation".

Point: He is frightened but keeps his feelings controlled. Evidence: he reports terrible news in short, flat sentences — "Not damaged — gone" — and says "Nobody will tell me about Mrs Thompson, which means I already know", instead of saying he is scared. Any point + a matching quotation = 2 marks.
